OpenFNO · Open-source algo trading desk · Indian F&O · self-hosted

Not a black box.

OpenFNO is a trading engine you can see inside: every tick stored, every rule written down, every rupee accounted for — running on your own machine, with your own broker keys.

Read the docs · Read the source on GitHub

Data

Dhan is the main live feed and option chain recorder, FYERS the backup feed and the source of history backfills, and TrueData a third feed; Angel One is connected read only, for market movers and linked portfolios. Ticks, minute bars and option-chain snapshots go into one store, compressed after two hours and archived every night. This deployment's store holds five years of index candles, 1 minute to daily, and minute-level option history around the at-the-money strike for NIFTY, BANKNIFTY and SENSEX. Every picker in the console lists what is stored before you choose a range.

Option chain

Calls one side, puts the other, the strike ladder between them. Live, with Greeks, build-up, PCR, max pain and the walls — and the same chain rebuilt for any past minute from stored history, which is what makes an option strategy testable at all.

Strategy

A setup you write in the console is a list of conditions — price against VWAP or an EMA, an EMA pair, RSI, candle body, Supertrend, ADX, the opening range, the gap. Every bar passes through them; if they all hold, there is a signal. Beside it, the catalogue holds 25 strategies, each specified from its own code, and one on_bar contract runs them all, live and in replay.

Risk

A limit on the leg, on the group and on the day — editable while the run is going, measured per trading day — and a kill switch that survives a restart. Every stop says why it stopped: guard, limit, market close, you, or the end of the range. Execution is paper, on real ticks, in lots × lot size.

The console

Six screens, no mock-ups: captures of this deployment, as it ran.

Market pulse
Indices, the nearest MCX contracts and the large caps, each with its day range and how old its last price is. A stale price says so.
Option chain
Calls left, puts right, strike in the middle; open interest and its change, IV, and what the pair of them means. Stamped with when it was captured and by which vendor.
History on hand
What is actually on disk — index, resolution, first and last session, bars, and whether they came from a backfill or the live feed — before you are asked to pick a range.
Setup editor
A long setup and a short setup written as conditions, under the history the replay will read: each resolution's stored sessions, the range, the lots and the square-off time.
Market factors
Walls, max pain, PCR, ATM IV, participant open interest and flows — each stamped with how fresh it is and with what our own testing found, including "not tested yet".
A finished run
Net P&L, win rate, profit factor, drawdown — and the account panel, because a total says nothing about whether the account survived to collect it. Strategy name hidden.

The only run we show in full lost money.

A directional strategy replayed over BANKNIFTY one-minute candles, 176 sessions from January to September 2026, every option leg priced from stored premiums. Entries that could not be priced are listed as skipped, never filled at a made-up price.

Net −₹1,05,391 on a ₹10,00,000 account: 760 closed trades, 35% winners, −₹139 a trade, 75 of 176 days positive, and a deepest fall of ₹1,58,431.

No strategy here has shown an edge after costs, and everything runs on paper.

Your machine. Your keys.

A .NET 10 API, a Python engine, a React console, TimescaleDB and Redis — on hardware you control, with your own broker keys. The whole engine is on GitHub, and the local deployment guide takes it from a fresh clone to a running desk on Windows, macOS or Linux.
